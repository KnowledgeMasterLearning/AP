Macroeconomics

Course Overview

AP MACROECONOMICS · COURSE OVERVIEW

AP Macroeconomics is divided into 6 units, built around four core concepts: Measuring Economic Performance, Economic Fluctuations and Stabilization Policies, Financial Sector and Monetary Policy, Open Economy and International Finance.

Units and Exam Weighting

Unit 1Basic Economic Concepts5–10%
Unit 2Economic Indicators and the Business Cycle12–17%
Unit 3National Income and Price Determination17–27%
Unit 4Financial Sector18–23%
Unit 5Long-Run Consequences of Stabilization Policies20–30%
Unit 6Open Economy—International Trade and Finance10–13%
Note: Units 3, 4, and 5 together account for about 55–80% of the exam. Units 4 and 5 alone make up nearly half — prioritize them.

Unit 1 · Basic Economic Concepts

5–10%

Establishes the analytical foundation of macroeconomics — scarcity, opportunity cost, and comparative advantage.

  • 1.1 ScarcityLimited resources vs. unlimited wants; the fundamental economic problem
  • 1.2 Opportunity Cost and the PPCDrawing and interpreting the PPC; opportunity cost, efficiency, growth
  • 1.3 Comparative Advantage and Gains from TradeAbsolute vs. comparative advantage; gains from specialization; terms of trade
  • 1.4 DemandLaw of demand; downward-sloping demand curve; change in demand vs. quantity demanded
  • 1.5 SupplyLaw of supply; upward-sloping supply curve; change in supply vs. quantity supplied
  • 1.6 Market Equilibrium, Disequilibrium, and ChangesDetermining equilibrium; shortages and surpluses; effects of shifts

Unit 2 · Economic Indicators and the Business Cycle

12–17%

How to measure a nation's economic performance — GDP, unemployment, and inflation.

  • 2.1 The Circular Flow and GDPDefinition of GDP; circular flow; three methods of measuring GDP; nominal GDP
  • 2.2 Limitations of GDPGDP does not count non-market transactions, underground economy, environmental quality, or quality of life
  • 2.3 UnemploymentLabor force, unemployment rate, labor force participation; types (frictional, structural, cyclical); natural rate
  • 2.4 Price Indices and InflationCPI definition and calculation; inflation rate; real variables; CPI biases
  • 2.5 Costs of InflationUnanticipated inflation's effects on borrowers and lenders; menu costs; shoe-leather costs
  • 2.6 Real v. Nominal GDPDifference between nominal and real GDP; GDP deflator
  • 2.7 Business CyclesPhases (expansion, recession) and turning points (peak, trough); output gap; potential GDP

Unit 3 · National Income and Price Determination

17–27%

Builds the AD-AS model to analyze economic fluctuations and short-run stabilization policy.

  • 3.1 Aggregate Demand (AD)Definition and downward slope (wealth, interest rate, exchange rate effects); shifters of AD
  • 3.2 MultipliersSpending and tax multipliers; MPC and MPS
  • 3.3 Short-Run Aggregate Supply (SRAS)Definition and upward slope (sticky wages); shifters of SRAS
  • 3.4 Long-Run Aggregate Supply (LRAS)Vertical LRAS; potential GDP; short-run vs. long-run
  • 3.5 Equilibrium in the AD-AS ModelShort-run and long-run equilibrium; recessionary and inflationary gaps
  • 3.6 Changes in the AD-AS ModelDemand and supply shocks; demand-pull and cost-push inflation
  • 3.7 Long-Run Self-AdjustmentFlexible wages and prices restore full employment
  • 3.8 Fiscal PolicyExpansionary and contractionary fiscal policy; government spending and taxes on AD; lags
  • 3.9 Automatic StabilizersProgressive income tax and transfer payments stabilize the economy

Unit 4 · Financial Sector

18–23%

Money, banking, and how central banks use monetary policy to influence the economy.

  • 4.1 Financial AssetsDefinition and types; stocks vs. bonds
  • 4.2 Nominal v. Real Interest RatesFisher equation; difference between nominal and real rates
  • 4.3 Definition, Measurement, and Functions of MoneyThree functions of money; M1 and M2; monetary base
  • 4.4 Banking and the Expansion of the Money SupplyBalance sheets; required and excess reserves; money multiplier; money creation
  • 4.5 The Money MarketMoney demand and supply; equilibrium interest rate; monetary policy effects on rates
  • 4.6 Monetary PolicyExpansionary and contractionary monetary policy; open market operations; reserve rate; policy rate
  • 4.7 The Loanable Funds MarketSupply and demand of loanable funds; interest rate; crowding out

Unit 5 · Long-Run Consequences of Stabilization Policies

20–30%

Long-run effects of stabilization policy — the Phillips curve and economic growth.

  • 5.1 Fiscal and Monetary Policy ActionsShort-run effects of policy on AD, output, prices, and interest rates
  • 5.2 The Phillips CurveShort-run and long-run Phillips curve; inflation-unemployment tradeoff; natural rate; shifts of SRPC
  • 5.3 Money Growth and InflationQuantity theory of money; MV = PQ; money supply growth and inflation
  • 5.4 Government Deficits and the National DebtDeficits and surpluses; national debt; effects on interest rates and investment
  • 5.5 Crowding OutHow government borrowing raises interest rates and reduces private investment
  • 5.6 Economic GrowthSources of long-run growth; physical and human capital; technology; saving and investment
  • 5.7 Public Policy and Economic GrowthPolicies that promote growth: education, infrastructure, R&D, property rights

Unit 6 · Open Economy

10–13%

International trade, exchange rates, and international capital flows in an open economy.

  • 6.1 Balance of Payments AccountsCurrent account and capital/financial account; BOP identity
  • 6.2 Exchange RatesDefinition; appreciation and depreciation
  • 6.3 The Foreign Exchange MarketSupply and demand in the FX market; equilibrium exchange rate
  • 6.4 Effect of Policy and Economic Conditions on FX MarketEffects of interest rates, inflation, and capital flows on exchange rates
  • 6.5 Changes in FX Market and Net ExportsEffects of exchange rate changes on net exports and AD
  • 6.6 Real Interest Rates and International Capital FlowsHow real interest rate differentials affect capital flows and exchange rates
