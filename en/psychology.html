Course Overview

AP PSYCHOLOGY · COURSE OVERVIEW

AP Psychology is divided into 5 units, built around psychology as a science: Biological Bases of Behavior, Cognition, Development and Learning, Social Psychology and Personality, Mental and Physical Health. The course emphasizes scientific methodology, critical thinking, and a systematic understanding of behavior and mental processes.

Units and Exam Weighting

Unit 1Biological Bases of Behavior15–25%
Unit 2Cognition15–25%
Unit 3Development and Learning15–25%
Unit 4Social Psychology and Personality15–25%
Unit 5Mental and Physical Health15–25%
Note: All five units carry equal weight (15–25% each). The exam doesn't favor any one unit, so every unit must be mastered systematically.

Unit 1 · Biological Bases of Behavior

15–25%

Builds the biological foundation of psychology — the starting point for understanding all behavior and mental processes.

  • 1.1 Interaction of Heredity and EnvironmentTwin and adoption studies; heritability; nature vs. nurture
  • 1.2 Overview of the Nervous SystemCNS and PNS; somatic and autonomic nervous systems; sympathetic and parasympathetic
  • 1.3 The Neuron and Neural FiringNeuron structure; action potential; all-or-none law; refractory period; neurotransmitters; psychoactive drugs
  • 1.4 The BrainBrainstem, cerebellum, limbic system, cerebral cortex; four lobes; Broca's and Wernicke's areas; split-brain research; plasticity; brain imaging
  • 1.5 SleepCircadian rhythms; sleep stages; REM sleep; dream theories; sleep disorders
  • 1.6 SensationSensory thresholds; visual and auditory systems; chemical senses; touch; pain; vestibular and kinesthetic senses

Unit 2 · Cognition

15–25%

Focuses on how humans acquire, process, store, and retrieve information.

  • 2.1 PerceptionBottom-up vs. top-down processing; Gestalt principles; selective attention; depth perception; perceptual constancy; illusions
  • 2.2 Thinking, Problem-Solving, Judgments, and Decision-MakingConcepts and prototypes; schemas; algorithms vs. heuristics; representative and availability heuristics; framing; sunk-cost fallacy
  • 2.3 Introduction to MemoryExplicit vs. implicit; episodic vs. semantic; procedural; working memory; multi-store model; levels of processing
  • 2.4 Encoding MemoriesEncoding types; mnemonics; chunking; spacing effect; serial position effect
  • 2.5 Storing MemoriesSensory, short-term, long-term memory; maintenance vs. elaborative rehearsal; autobiographical memory; memory disorders
  • 2.6 Retrieving MemoriesRecall vs. recognition; context-dependent memory; mood-congruent memory; state-dependent memory; retrieval practice and testing effect
  • 2.7 Forgetting and Other Memory ChallengesForgetting curve; encoding failure; proactive and retroactive interference; tip-of-the-tongue; repression; misinformation effect; source amnesia
  • 2.8 Intelligence and AchievementTheories of intelligence; intelligence tests; reliability and validity; heredity and environment

Unit 3 · Development and Learning

15–25%

Explores human development across the lifespan and systematically introduces core theories of learning.

  • 3.1 Themes and MethodsStability and change; nature and nurture; continuity and stages; cross-sectional vs. longitudinal studies
  • 3.2 Physical DevelopmentPrenatal development; teratogens; infant reflexes; visual cliff; puberty; physical changes in adulthood
  • 3.3 Gender and Sexual OrientationSex vs. gender; gender socialization; gender identity; gender schema theory; sexual orientation
  • 3.4 Cognitive DevelopmentPiaget's four stages; schemas; assimilation and accommodation; Vygotsky's sociocultural theory; crystallized vs. fluid intelligence
  • 3.5 Communication and Language DevelopmentLanguage structure (phonemes, morphemes, semantics, grammar); language stages; overgeneralization
  • 3.6 Social-Emotional DevelopmentEcological systems theory; parenting styles; attachment types; Erikson's eight stages; Marcia's identity statuses
  • 3.7 Classical ConditioningPavlov's experiments; unconditioned and conditioned stimuli/responses; acquisition, extinction, spontaneous recovery; generalization and discrimination; taste aversion
  • 3.8 Operant ConditioningLaw of effect; positive/negative reinforcement and punishment; shaping; schedules of reinforcement; learned helplessness
  • 3.9 Social, Cognitive, and Neurological FactorsObservational learning; Bandura's Bobo doll; insight learning; latent learning; cognitive maps

Unit 4 · Social Psychology and Personality

15–25%

Explores how individuals think about, influence, and relate to others, and how personality forms and is assessed.

  • 4.1 Attribution Theory and Person PerceptionAttribution theory; fundamental attribution error; actor-observer bias; self-serving bias; locus of control; self-fulfilling prophecy; social comparison; mere exposure effect
  • 4.2 Attitude Formation and Attitude ChangeStereotypes; implicit attitudes; prejudice and discrimination; belief perseverance; cognitive dissonance
  • 4.3 Psychology of Social SituationsSocial norms; normative and informational influence; central and peripheral routes to persuasion; conformity; obedience; group polarization; groupthink; bystander effect; altruism
  • 4.4 Psychodynamic and Humanistic TheoriesFreud's structural model (id, ego, superego); defense mechanisms; projective tests; humanism; self-actualization; unconditional positive regard
  • 4.5 Social-Cognitive and Trait TheoriesReciprocal determinism; self-efficacy; Big Five; factor analysis
  • 4.6 MotivationDrive-reduction theory; arousal theory; Maslow's hierarchy; intrinsic vs. extrinsic motivation
  • 4.7 EmotionJames-Lange; Cannon-Bard; Schachter-Singer two-factor theory; facial feedback hypothesis

Unit 5 · Mental and Physical Health

15–25%

Focuses on defining mental health, classifying and treating psychological disorders, and positive psychology.

  • 5.1 Introduction to Health PsychologyStress; stressors; coping; stress and physical health
  • 5.2 Positive PsychologyWell-being; resilience; character strengths; mindfulness
  • 5.3 Explaining and Classifying DisordersDefining psychological disorders; DSM; biopsychosocial model
  • 5.4 Categories of Psychological DisordersAnxiety disorders; OCD; PTSD; depression; bipolar; schizophrenia; personality disorders
  • 5.5 Treatment of DisordersPsychotherapy (psychoanalytic, humanistic, behavioral, cognitive, CBT); biomedical treatment (medication, ECT, TMS)
