Statistics

Course Overview

AP STATISTICS · COURSE OVERVIEW

AP Statistics is divided into 5 units, built around four core concepts: Exploring Data, Probability and Random Variables, Statistical Inference, Relationships Between Variables.

Units and Exam Weighting

Unit 1Exploring One-Variable Data and Collecting Data20–30%
Unit 2Probability, Random Variables, and Probability Distributions15–25%
Unit 3Inference for Categorical Data: Proportions15–25%
Unit 4Inference for Quantitative Data: Means10–20%
Unit 5Regression Analysis10–20%
Note: Unit 1 carries the highest weight (20–30%) and is the foundation of the course. Units 3 and 4 together account for about 25–45% of the exam — the core of statistical inference and a frequent source of FRQ.

Unit 1 · Exploring One-Variable Data and Collecting Data

20–30%

Establishes statistical thinking and data analysis foundations, while covering methods of data collection.

  • 1.1 Introducing StatisticsComponents of a statistical study; population vs. sample; parameter vs. statistic; meaning of "in context"
  • 1.2 VariablesObservational unit; variable; categorical vs. quantitative; discrete vs. continuous
  • 1.3 Tabular Representation for One Categorical VariableFrequency table; relative frequency table; proportions, percentages, ratios
  • 1.4 Graphical Representations for One Categorical VariableBar chart; pie chart; using graphs to support conclusions; comparing datasets
  • 1.5 Graphical Representations for One Quantitative VariableHistogram; stem-and-leaf; dotplot; effect of bin width
  • 1.6 Descriptions for One Quantitative VariableSOCS framework; skewed left/right; unimodal/bimodal/uniform; outliers, gaps, clusters
  • 1.7 Summary Statistics for One Quantitative VariableMean, median, Q1, Q3, percentile; range, IQR, SD, variance; outlier rules; resistant vs. non-resistant
  • 1.8 Graphical Representations of Summary StatisticsFive-number summary; boxplot; mean vs. median relation to shape
  • 1.9 Comparisons of DistributionsCompare shape, center, spread, outliers; back-to-back stem plot; side-by-side boxplot; z-score
  • 1.10 The Investigative Question RevisitedThree components of an investigative question; census; experiment vs. observational study; prospective vs. retrospective; confounding variable
  • 1.11 Random SamplingSampling with/without replacement; SRS; stratified; cluster; systematic; when to use each
  • 1.12 Potential Problems with SamplingBias; voluntary response bias; undercoverage; nonresponse; response bias
  • 1.13 Experimental DesignExperiment components; random assignment and causal inference; control, placebo, blinding; completely randomized, randomized block, matched pairs

Unit 2 · Probability, Random Variables, and Probability Distributions

15–25%

Builds the foundation of probability theory — the theoretical support for later statistical inference.

  • 2.1 Distributions of Two Categorical VariablesTwo-way table; joint/marginal/conditional relative frequency; side-by-side bar chart; segmented bar chart; mosaic plot; association
  • 2.2 Summary Statistics for Two Categorical VariablesJoint, marginal, and conditional relative frequency; using conditional distributions to support conclusions
  • 2.3 Estimating Probabilities Using SimulationRandom process; outcome; event; simulation steps; law of large numbers
  • 2.4 Introduction to ProbabilitySample space; theoretical probability; range of probability; complement rule
  • 2.5 Mutually Exclusive EventsJoint probability; intersection; mutually exclusive condition P(A∩B) = 0
  • 2.6 Conditional ProbabilityP(A|B) = P(A∩B)/P(B); general multiplication rule P(A∩B) = P(A)·P(B|A)
  • 2.7 Independent Events and UnionsIndependence condition P(A∩B) = P(A)·P(B); independent vs. mutually exclusive; general addition rule
  • 2.8 Introduction to Random VariablesRandom variable; discrete random variable; probability distribution conditions; cumulative distribution
  • 2.9 Parameters of Random VariablesExpected value μ_X = ΣxᵢP(xᵢ); standard deviation; variance; interpret in context
  • 2.10 The Binomial DistributionBINS conditions; binomial probability formula; mean μ_X = np, σ_X = √(np(1−p)); simulation estimates
  • 2.11 The Normal DistributionContinuous random variable; normal distribution properties; standard normal; empirical rule (68–95–99.7); z-score probabilities; boundary values
  • 2.12 Sampling Distributions and CLTSampling distribution; simulation; randomization distribution; central limit theorem; effect of sample size

Unit 3 · Inference for Categorical Data: Proportions

15–25%

Focuses on statistical inference for one- and two-sample proportion problems.

  • 3.1 EstimatorsPoint estimator; unbiased estimator; sample proportion p̂ as an estimator of p
  • 3.2 Sampling Distributions for Sample ProportionsMean and SD of p̂; normal approximation conditions (np ≥ 10, n(1−p) ≥ 10)
  • 3.3 Constructing a Confidence IntervalStructure of CI; critical value; margin of error; interpreting confidence level
  • 3.4 Justifying a ClaimUsing a CI to test a claim; interpreting in context
  • 3.5 Setting Up a TestNull H₀; alternative Hₐ; one-sided vs. two-sided
  • 3.6 p-ValuesDefinition and interpretation; significance level α; decisions using p-value
  • 3.7 Carrying Out a TestTest statistic z; compute p-value; full conclusion
  • 3.8 Potential ErrorsType I; Type II; power; relationship between α and β
  • 3.9 Sampling Distributions for DifferenceMean and SD of p̂₁ − p̂₂; normal approximation conditions
  • 3.10 CI for DifferenceComputing and interpreting the CI
  • 3.11 Justifying a ClaimWhether the CI contains 0
  • 3.12 Setting Up a Test for DifferenceH₀: p₁ − p₂ = 0; pooled proportion
  • 3.13 Carrying Out a Test for DifferenceTest statistic; p-value; conclusion
  • 3.14 Setting Up a Chi-Square TestExpected counts; chi-square statistic; degrees of freedom
  • 3.15 Carrying Out a Chi-Square TestCompute chi-square; p-value; conclusion and interpretation

Unit 4 · Inference for Quantitative Data: Means

10–20%

Focuses on statistical inference for one- and two-sample mean problems.

  • 4.1 Sampling Distributions for Sample MeansMean and SD of x̄; central limit theorem; normality conditions
  • 4.2 Constructing a Confidence Intervalt-distribution; degrees of freedom; computing and interpreting CI
  • 4.3 Justifying a ClaimInterpreting a CI in context
  • 4.4 Setting Up a TestH₀ and Hₐ; one-sample t-test
  • 4.5 Carrying Out a Testt-statistic; p-value; conclusion
  • 4.6 Sampling Distributions for DifferenceMean and SD of x̄₁ − x̄₂; conditions
  • 4.7 CI for DifferenceTwo-sample t-interval; computation and interpretation
  • 4.8 Justifying a ClaimWhether the CI contains 0
  • 4.9 Setting Up a Test for DifferenceH₀: μ₁ − μ₂ = 0; two-sample t-test
  • 4.10 Carrying Out a Test for Differencet-statistic; p-value; conclusion

Unit 5 · Regression Analysis

10–20%

Examines relationships between two quantitative variables; builds and evaluates linear regression models.

  • 5.1 Graphical RepresentationsScatterplot; direction, form, strength, outliers; explanatory vs. response variable
  • 5.2 CorrelationCorrelation coefficient r; properties of r; correlation ≠ causation
  • 5.3 Linear Regression ModelsLeast-squares regression line; interpreting slope and intercept; prediction
  • 5.4 ResidualsDefinition and computation; residual plot; judging whether a linear model is appropriate
  • 5.5 Least-Squares RegressionLeast-squares criterion; computing the regression line; interpreting r²; outliers and influential points
