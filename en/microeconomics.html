Microeconomics

Course Overview

AP MICROECONOMICS · COURSE OVERVIEW

AP Microeconomics is divided into 6 units, built around four core concepts: Scarcity, Cost-Benefit & Marginal Analysis, Production and Resource Allocation, Market Efficiency and Government Policy.

Units and Exam Weighting

Unit 1Basic Economic Concepts12–15%
Unit 2Supply and Demand20–25%
Unit 3Production, Cost, and the Perfect Competition Model22–25%
Unit 4Imperfect Competition15–22%
Unit 5Factor Markets10–13%
Unit 6Market Failure and the Role of Government8–13%
Note: Units 2 and 3 together account for about 42–50% of the exam, making them the most important part of the course.

Unit 1 · Basic Economic Concepts

12–15%

Establishes the economic way of thinking — the foundation of the whole course.

  • 1.1 ScarcityLimited resources vs. unlimited wants; scarcity as the fundamental economic problem
  • 1.2 Resource Allocation and Economic SystemsThree basic economic systems; "what, how, and for whom to produce"
  • 1.3 Production Possibilities CurveDrawing and interpreting the PPC; opportunity cost, efficiency, growth
  • 1.4 Comparative Advantage and TradeAbsolute vs. comparative advantage; gains from specialization and trade
  • 1.5 Cost-Benefit AnalysisThe decision rule: act when marginal benefit ≥ marginal cost
  • 1.6 Marginal Analysis and Consumer ChoiceDiminishing marginal utility; utility maximization MU₁/P₁ = MU₂/P₂

Unit 2 · Supply and Demand

20–25%

The core analytical toolkit of microeconomics, on which all later market analysis is built.

  • 2.1 DemandLaw of demand; downward-sloping demand curve; change in demand vs. quantity demanded
  • 2.2 SupplyLaw of supply; upward-sloping supply curve; change in supply vs. quantity supplied
  • 2.3 Price Elasticity of DemandMidpoint formula; elastic, inelastic, unit elastic; elasticity and total revenue
  • 2.4 Price Elasticity of SupplyFormula; types of elasticity; factors affecting supply elasticity
  • 2.5 Other ElasticitiesCross-price elasticity; income elasticity
  • 2.6 Market Equilibrium and SurplusDetermining equilibrium; consumer and producer surplus
  • 2.7 Market Disequilibrium and Changes in EquilibriumShortages and surpluses; effects of shifts on price and quantity
  • 2.8 Government InterventionPrice ceilings and floors; taxes and subsidies; deadweight loss; tax incidence
  • 2.9 International Trade and Public PolicyTariffs and quotas; effects of trade on surplus

Unit 3 · Production, Cost, and the Perfect Competition Model

22–25%

Starts from firm production decisions, builds the cost curves, and derives short- and long-run equilibrium under perfect competition.

  • 3.1 The Production FunctionShort-run production; total, marginal, and average product; diminishing marginal returns
  • 3.2 Short-Run Production CostsFixed, variable, and total cost; marginal, average total, average variable, average fixed cost
  • 3.3 Long-Run Production CostsEconomies and diseconomies of scale; LRATC; minimum efficient scale
  • 3.4 Types of ProfitAccounting vs. economic profit; explicit and implicit costs; normal profit
  • 3.5 Profit MaximizationProfit-maximizing rule MR = MC
  • 3.6 Short-Run and Long-Run DecisionsShutdown point (P < min AVC); break-even (P = min ATC); entry and exit
  • 3.7 Perfect CompetitionMarket characteristics; horizontal demand curve; short- and long-run equilibrium; allocative and productive efficiency

Unit 4 · Imperfect Competition

15–22%

Analyzes monopoly, monopolistic competition, and oligopoly — how firms use market power to price and the resulting efficiency losses.

  • 4.1 Introduction to Imperfectly Competitive MarketsTypes of imperfect competition; barriers to entry; P > MC → allocative inefficiency
  • 4.2 MonopolyCauses of monopoly; MR < P; profit max MR = MC; monopoly price and quantity; deadweight loss; natural monopoly
  • 4.3 Price DiscriminationConditions; perfect price discrimination: P = MC, zero consumer surplus
  • 4.4 Monopolistic CompetitionProduct differentiation; short- and long-run equilibrium; zero long-run economic profit; excess capacity
  • 4.5 Oligopoly and Game TheoryInterdependence; collusion and cartels; dominant strategy; Nash equilibrium; prisoner's dilemma

Unit 5 · Factor Markets

10–13%

Applies supply and demand to labor and other factor markets — how firms decide how much to hire and what wage to pay.

  • 5.1 Introduction to Factor MarketsDerived demand; marginal revenue product (MRP); marginal factor cost (MFC)
  • 5.2 Changes in Factor Demand and SupplyFactors shifting factor demand and supply; equilibrium wage and employment
  • 5.3 Profit-Maximizing Behavior in Perfectly Competitive Factor MarketsHiring rule MRP = MFC; labor market equilibrium
  • 5.4 Monopsonistic MarketsMonopsony characteristics; MFC > wage; lower wage and employment than competitive; deadweight loss

Unit 6 · Market Failure and Government

8–13%

Analyzes when markets fail to achieve efficiency and how government intervention can correct it.

  • 6.1 Efficient and Inefficient OutcomesSocial optimum MSB = MSC; types of market failure; deadweight loss
  • 6.2 ExternalitiesNegative (pollution) and positive (education) externalities; corrective taxes and subsidies
  • 6.3 Public and Private GoodsExcludability and rivalry; public goods; free-rider problem; common resources; tragedy of the commons
  • 6.4 Government Intervention in Different Market StructuresEffects of taxes, subsidies, and price controls in different structures; antitrust policy
  • 6.5 InequalityMeasuring income and wealth inequality; Lorenz curve and Gini coefficient; redistribution policy
