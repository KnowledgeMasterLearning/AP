Biology

Course Overview

AP BIOLOGY · COURSE OVERVIEW

AP Biology is divided into 8 units, built around four core concepts: Evolution, Energetics, Information Storage and Transmission, Systems Interactions.

Units and Exam Weighting

Unit 1Chemistry of Life8–11%
Unit 2Cells10–13%
Unit 3Cellular Energetics12–16%
Unit 4Cell Communication and Cell Cycle10–15%
Unit 5Heredity8–11%
Unit 6Gene Expression and Regulation12–16%
Unit 7Natural Selection13–20%
Unit 8Ecology10–15%
Note: Unit 7 (Natural Selection) carries the highest weight and is the course's central thread. Units 3 and 6 are tied for second — both are key units.

Unit 1 · Chemistry of Life

8–11%

Builds the chemistry foundation for biology — the structure and function of water, elements, and biological macromolecules.

  • 1.1 Structure of Water and Hydrogen BondingPolarity, hydrogen bonds, specific heat, solvent properties, cohesion and adhesion; water's importance to life
  • 1.2 Elements of LifeMain elements (CHNOPS); roles in biomolecules
  • 1.3 Introduction to MacromoleculesMonomers and polymers; dehydration synthesis and hydrolysis
  • 1.4 CarbohydratesMono-, di-, and polysaccharides; energy storage and structural support
  • 1.5 LipidsFatty acids, triglycerides, phospholipids, steroids; hydrophobic properties and membranes; energy storage
  • 1.6 Nucleic AcidsDNA and RNA structure; nucleotides; storage and transmission of genetic information
  • 1.7 ProteinsAmino acid structure; peptide bonds; four levels of protein structure; structure-function relationship; denaturation

Unit 2 · Cells

10–13%

Focuses on cell structure and function — especially membrane structure and transport mechanisms.

  • 2.1 Cell Structure and FunctionProkaryotic vs. eukaryotic cells; organelles; endosymbiotic theory
  • 2.2 Cell SizeSurface area-to-volume ratio; why small cells are more efficient
  • 2.3 Plasma MembranePhospholipid bilayer; fluid mosaic model; membrane protein functions
  • 2.4 Membrane PermeabilitySelective permeability; factors affecting transport
  • 2.5 Membrane TransportPassive vs. active transport; diffusion, osmosis, pumps
  • 2.6 Facilitated DiffusionChannel and carrier proteins; concentration gradient
  • 2.7 Tonicity and OsmoregulationHypertonic, hypotonic, isotonic solutions; cell volume changes; osmoregulation
  • 2.8 Mechanisms of TransportSodium-potassium pump; cotransport; endocytosis and exocytosis
  • 2.9 Cell CompartmentalizationFunctions of the endomembrane system in eukaryotes
  • 2.10 Origins of Cell CompartmentalizationEndosymbiotic theory; evolution of membrane systems

Unit 3 · Cellular Energetics

12–16%

How cells acquire and use energy — enzymes, photosynthesis, and cellular respiration.

  • 3.1 EnzymesEnzyme structure and function; active site; catalysis; factors affecting activity
  • 3.2 Environmental Impacts on Enzyme FunctionEffects of temperature, pH, substrate concentration, and inhibitors
  • 3.3 Cellular EnergyATP structure and function; energy coupling; redox reactions
  • 3.4 PhotosynthesisLight reactions and Calvin cycle; light energy to chemical energy; chloroplast structure
  • 3.5 Cellular RespirationGlycolysis, citric acid cycle, oxidative phosphorylation; mitochondria; ATP yield

Unit 4 · Cell Communication and Cell Cycle

10–15%

How cells receive and respond to signals, and how they divide and regulate the cell cycle.

  • 4.1 Cell CommunicationTypes of signaling; local and long-distance communication
  • 4.2 Introduction to Signal TransductionReception, transduction, response
  • 4.3 Signal Transduction PathwaysReceptor types; second messengers; phosphorylation cascades
  • 4.4 FeedbackNegative and positive feedback; roles in biological systems
  • 4.5 Cell CycleInterphase and mitotic phase; stages of mitosis; cell cycle regulation
  • 4.6 Regulation of Cell CycleCheckpoints; cyclins and CDKs; cancer and loss of cycle control

Unit 5 · Heredity

8–11%

How genetic information is transmitted — from meiosis to Mendelian genetics.

  • 5.1 MeiosisStages of meiosis; homologous chromosome separation; gamete formation
  • 5.2 Meiosis and Genetic DiversityCrossing over; independent assortment; sources of genetic variation
  • 5.3 Mendelian GeneticsDominant and recessive; law of segregation; law of independent assortment; Punnett squares
  • 5.4 Non-Mendelian GeneticsIncomplete dominance, codominance, multiple alleles, polygenic inheritance, linked genes
  • 5.5 Environmental Effects on PhenotypePhenotype = genotype + environment; environmental effects on gene expression

Unit 6 · Gene Expression and Regulation

12–16%

Molecular-level study of how DNA is replicated, transcribed, translated, and regulated.

  • 6.1 DNA and RNA StructureNucleotide structure; double helix; RNA types and functions
  • 6.2 DNA ReplicationSemiconservative replication; replication fork; key enzymes (helicase, polymerase, ligase)
  • 6.3 Transcription and RNA ProcessingTranscription mechanism; mRNA processing (capping, tailing, splicing)
  • 6.4 TranslationGenetic code; ribosomes; tRNA; protein synthesis
  • 6.5 Regulation of Gene ExpressionProkaryotic operon model; eukaryotic transcriptional regulation; epigenetics
  • 6.6 Gene Expression and Cell SpecializationCell differentiation; tissue-specific gene expression
  • 6.7 MutationsPoint mutations and frameshift mutations; effects on protein function
  • 6.8 BiotechnologyPCR, gel electrophoresis, DNA sequencing, CRISPR, and other tools and applications

Unit 7 · Natural Selection

13–20%

The course's central thread — the mechanisms and evidence of evolution.

  • 7.1 Introduction to Natural SelectionDarwin's theory; basic principles of natural selection
  • 7.2 Natural SelectionVariation, inheritance, selection pressure; adaptation and evolution
  • 7.3 Artificial SelectionHuman-directed selection; comparison with natural selection
  • 7.4 Population GeneticsGene pool; allele frequencies; Hardy-Weinberg principle
  • 7.5 Hardy-Weinberg EquilibriumConditions for equilibrium; evolutionary forces that cause deviation
  • 7.6 Evidence of EvolutionFossil record, anatomy, molecular biology, biogeography
  • 7.7 Common AncestryPhylogenetic trees; homologous vs. analogous structures
  • 7.8 Continuing EvolutionAntibiotic resistance, pesticide resistance, and other modern examples
  • 7.9 PhylogenyConstructing and interpreting phylogenetic trees; molecular clocks
  • 7.10 SpeciationGeographic and reproductive isolation; sympatric and allopatric speciation
  • 7.11 Variations in PopulationsGenetic drift, gene flow, mutation, and selection
  • 7.12 Origins of Life on EarthEarly Earth conditions; hypotheses on the origin of life; RNA world

Unit 8 · Ecology

10–15%

Interactions between organisms and their environment — from individuals to ecosystems.

  • 8.1 Responses to the EnvironmentAnimal behavior; taxis, migration, biological rhythms
  • 8.2 Energy Flow Through EcosystemsFood chains and webs; trophic levels; energy pyramid; 10% rule
  • 8.3 Population EcologyPopulation growth models (exponential, logistic); carrying capacity
  • 8.4 Effect of Density on PopulationsDensity-dependent and density-independent factors
  • 8.5 Community EcologySpecies interactions (competition, predation, symbiosis); niche; community structure
  • 8.6 BiodiversityGenetic, species, and ecosystem diversity; factors affecting biodiversity
  • 8.7 Disruptions in EcosystemsHuman impacts; invasive species; habitat destruction; conservation biology
